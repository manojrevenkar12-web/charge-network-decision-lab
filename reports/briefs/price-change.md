# The 2025 price increase

**What did the 2025 price increase do to sessions and revenue?**

It cost between 1% and 10% of sessions at the raised sites, and raised their revenue by 7% to 18%.

## Decision

Keep the higher price. Revenue rises at every effect size the data allows, so the decision does not depend on the exact number.

## What this rests on

- 290 raised sites against 408 unchanged ones over two years of weekly data.
- The usual comparisons say sessions went up (+4.1% before and after, +1.5% with two-way fixed effects): the raised sites were growing faster.
- The weeks before each increase show it: the raised sites pulled ahead by 0.18% a week before anything changed (p = 1e-08).
- Two methods that allow for that disagree in a known direction: controls reweighted to grow like the raised sites (synthetic DiD) give the smaller loss, each site's own growth continued (trend imputation) the larger one.

## What would change it

- A randomised price test, which would narrow the range to a single number (question 2 shows how).
- Evidence that growth at the raised sites was about to slow sharply, which would move the answer towards the smaller loss.

## Method

Before/after, two-way fixed effects, imputation (Borusyak, Jaravel and Spiess), event study, synthetic DiD (Arkhangelsky et al.), capacity-aware trend imputation; site bootstrap.
